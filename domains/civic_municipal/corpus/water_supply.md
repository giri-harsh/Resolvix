# Civic Complaint Registration — Bengaluru

## A note on governance structure

Bengaluru's civic administration changed structurally in the recent past, and this matters for routing complaints correctly. The Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP), which had governed the city as a single municipal body since 2007, was formally dissolved on 2 September 2025. In its place, the Greater Bengaluru Governance Act, 2024 established the Greater Bengaluru Authority (GBA) as an apex coordinating body, with day-to-day civic services now split across five newly created city corporations covering North, South, East, West, and Central Bengaluru. Despite this restructuring, the citizen-facing complaint portals and helpline numbers that existed under BBMP — Sahaaya, the 1533 helpline, and the e-Aasthi property portal — continued operating on the same infrastructure through the transition, and BBMP branding still appears on several official pages as of this writing. A citizen calling for help does not need to know which of the five corporations covers their address; the unified channels below still route correctly. Source: Wikipedia summary of the Greater Bengaluru Authority citing the Greater Bengaluru Governance Act, 2024, and multiple property-law explainer sites describing the September 2025 transition.

## Water supply complaints

Water supply and sewerage in Bengaluru is handled by the Bangalore Water Supply and Sewerage Board (BWSSB), an autonomous body that retained its identity through the BBMP-to-GBA transition. Citizens can report no water supply, contamination, low pressure, pipeline leaks, sewage overflow, manhole issues, or billing disputes through several channels: the 24x7 toll-free helpline 1916, the general helpline 080-2223 8888, WhatsApp messages to 8762228888, or the online Complaints Management System at cms.bwssb.gov.in. A separate number, 14420, is designated specifically for septic tank and sewer-line issues. Source: official BWSSB website (bwssb.karnataka.gov.in) contact page and BWSSB's own CMS portal.

BWSSB's citizen charter describes a three-level grievance redressal mechanism: a complaint is first logged with the call centre or portal, and if unresolved, it can be escalated to the area Executive Engineer and then further to the Engineer-in-Chief or the Board Chairman. **UNVERIFIED — needs confirmation:** the specific number of days BWSSB commits to for first-level resolution of a routine "no water supply" complaint was not found on an official source during this research and should not be quoted to a caller as a guaranteed timeline.

## Sanitation and garbage complaints

Solid waste collection, door-to-door garbage pickup, and illegal dumping complaints are handled through the same unified Sahaaya platform used for most other civic issues (see the Roads section below for the platform description). Complaints about uncollected garbage, waste dumping, or overflowing community bins can be logged the same way as pothole complaints — via the Sahaaya app/portal or the 1533 helpline — and are routed internally to the Solid Waste Management wing of the relevant city corporation. Multiple Deccan Herald reports from 2021–2023 describe citizens finding garbage and drainage complaints marked "resolved" on Sahaaya without any action having been taken on the ground, and describe a "Sahaaya 2.0" update that added a citizen-facing escalation/reopening feature specifically to address this. **UNVERIFIED — needs confirmation:** an official published SLA (e.g., "garbage complaints resolved within X hours") was not located; treat any specific number as unconfirmed rather than quoting it to a caller.

## Roads and potholes

Pothole and road-damage complaints in Bengaluru go through **Sahaaya**, officially rebranded as "Namma Bengaluru (Sahaaya 2.0)" — a unified grievance app that pools complaints across BBMP-successor road and engineering functions, BESCOM, BWSSB, BMTC, BMRCL, BMRDA, and BDA into one citizen-facing platform, allowing photo and video upload and day-by-day status tracking. Source: Karnataka government's official apps directory (apps.karnataka.gov.in) description of Sahaaya 2.0. A dedicated pothole-specific tool, "Fix My Street" (also referenced under a Kannada name, "Raste Gundi Gamana"), was separately launched as a GPS-enabled app that lets both officials and the public log and photograph potholes, assigning them to zone/ward engineers for accountability. Source: Deccan Herald reporting from multiple dates in 2022–2023.

Complaints can also be phoned in directly. During a 2023 tracking period, the civic body reported receiving pothole complaints through four parallel channels in roughly comparable volumes — phone calls, the Sahaaya app, WhatsApp, and the Sahaaya web portal — indicating no single channel is authoritative; citizens use whichever is convenient. Source: Deccan Herald, reporting on Deputy CM-directed pothole tracking, June 2023.

**Known reliability issue, disclosed rather than hidden:** multiple independent Deccan Herald investigations (2022, 2023) documented instances of pothole and drainage complaints being marked "resolved" on Sahaaya without ground verification, and BBMP officials publicly acknowledging capacity constraints, particularly during monsoon season. This is directly relevant to how a voice agent should hedge: it should not promise a specific resolution timeline it cannot verify, and should be prepared for a caller expressing frustration about a previously "closed" complaint — this is a documented, common experience, not an edge case.

## Streetlights

Responsibility for streetlights is **split between two agencies**, and getting this wrong sends a citizen's complaint nowhere: the civic corporation (formerly BBMP, now the relevant city corporation under GBA) owns and maintains the physical poles, fittings, and switching — so a broken pole, a lamp that stays on all day, or a non-functioning light should go through the same Sahaaya/1533 channel as roads and garbage. BESCOM (Bangalore Electricity Supply Company) is responsible for the electrical supply — cables, transformers, and wiring faults — and should be contacted separately for suspected power/wiring problems (e.g., a live wire, a transformer fault feeding a stretch of lights) via BESCOM's 24x7 helpline **1912**, WhatsApp 9449844640, or the emergency safety-issue numbers 9483191212 / 9483191222. Sources: BESCOM's official karnataka.gov.in contact page; a 2014 Deccan Herald report in which BESCOM explicitly stated that streetlight circuits are maintained by the civic corporation, not BESCOM, directing the public to the (then) BBMP complaint centre.

BESCOM's grievance process for general electricity complaints (outages, billing, meter faults) escalates through up to four levels if unresolved: the toll-free helpline, then the divisional Public Grievance Redressal (PGR) Cell, then the Consumer Grievance Redressal Forum (CGRF), and finally the Electricity Ombudsman of Karnataka. CGRF orders are typically issued within 30 to 60 working days depending on complaint type, per BESCOM's published citizen-charter description. Source: BESCOM citizen charter as summarized in independently published complaint guides referencing the CGRF process.
