# Application / Case Status Assistance — Karnataka & Bengaluru

## The Sakala framework — the backbone of status tracking in Karnataka

The single most important fact for this category: Karnataka operates under the **Karnataka Guarantee of Services to Citizens Act, 2011**, commonly known as **Sakala**, which is a Right-to-Public-Services law — Karnataka was the tenth Indian state to enact one. Under Sakala, whenever a citizen applies for any of a large, officially notified list of government services, they receive an acknowledgement slip bearing a unique **15-digit Guarantee of Services to Citizen (GSC) number**. As of the most recent official figure available (1 October 2024), Sakala covers **1,249 notified services across 107 institutions** — described by the Sakala Mission itself as the highest number of notified services under a Right to Service Act anywhere in the country. Source: sakala.kar.nic.in (official Sakala FAQ) and the National e-Governance Division's state-data directory entry for Karnataka.

Services explicitly listed as covered under the Act include: caste and income certificates (Revenue Department), residence certificates (Revenue Department), registration of births and deaths (Urban Development & Revenue Departments), driving licences (Transport Department), Khata transfer documents (Urban Development), issuance and modification of ration cards (Food Department), survey and settlement records (Revenue), house-plan sanction (Urban Local Bodies), and copies of FIRs/petitions (Police Department). Source: Wikipedia's summary of the Karnataka Sakala Services Act, cross-referenced against the official Sakala portal's own service-count framing.

**How status tracking actually works, in practice:** a citizen uses their 15-digit GSC number to check status directly on the Sakala website (sakala.kar.nic.in), or by sending the GSC number via SMS from their registered mobile phone to receive a status reply. If a service is not delivered within its notified time limit, or the application is rejected, the citizen has a right to file an appeal — first to a "Competent Officer," who can impose a cost on a defaulting/delaying public servant, and with a further right of appeal to a designated "Appellate Authority." Source: sakala.kar.nic.in official FAQ page, cross-referenced against the Karnataka Sakala Services Act's own legal summary.

The general Sakala helpline number is **080 4455 4455**. Source: National e-Governance Division state-data listing citing the Sakala Help Line.

## What this means for a specific application (e.g., a certificate or a civic complaint)

For certificate applications specifically (income, caste, domicile) processed through **Nadakacheri**, status is checked using a similar but portal-specific **RD (acknowledgement) number** — either by entering the RD number directly, or by verifying via mobile number and OTP and then selecting the relevant service from a dropdown of applications tied to that number. The Nadakacheri status page also displays which officer's approval is currently pending (e.g., Revenue Inspector, Tahsildar) and sends an SMS update to the registered mobile number at each stage of approval, with a final SMS and download link once the certificate is e-signed. Source: independently published Nadakacheri user guides describing the RD-number tracking flow, consistent across multiple sources.

For civic complaints (potholes, garbage, water, streetlights) logged through **Sahaaya**, status tracking works differently — Sahaaya generates its own complaint/ticket reference rather than a Sakala GSC number, and status ("open," "in progress," "resolved") is viewable within the app or portal itself. **Important honesty note for the assistant, not just a footnote:** multiple independent Deccan Herald investigations found cases where the Sahaaya status was marked "resolved" without verified ground action — so while the *mechanism* for checking status is straightforward, the assistant should not present a "resolved" status as proof the underlying issue is actually fixed, only as what the system currently records, and should offer to help the citizen reopen or escalate if they say the issue persists.

For **e-Khata / property-related applications** on the e-Aasthi portal, status is checked using the property's ePID (electronic Property Identification Number) or the owner's name, distinct from both the Sakala GSC and Nadakacheri RD number systems. See the certificates file (certificates.md) for e-Aasthi-specific detail.

## The general pattern worth encoding

A citizen asking "what's the status of my application" needs to be asked, gently, **which kind of application** — because the correct next question (GSC number vs. RD number vs. Sahaaya complaint ID vs. ePID) depends entirely on which government system issued the original reference number. This is a natural clarification point rather than something to guess at.
