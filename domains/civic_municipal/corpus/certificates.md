# Certificates & Documentation — Karnataka / Bengaluru

## Birth certificate

Birth registration in Karnataka is recorded through the eJanma system (the state's Registration of Births and Deaths platform), and certificates for events already registered in eJanma can be downloaded through the **Seva Sindhu** portal (sevasindhu.karnataka.gov.in) after payment of the applicable fee. Source: the official Office of the Chief Registrar of Births and Deaths portal (ejanma.karnataka.gov.in). To apply, a citizen creates or logs into a Seva Sindhu account (linkable to Aadhaar/DigiLocker), searches for "Application for Birth Certificate," and submits the request with supporting documents, most commonly the hospital discharge summary or birth intimation slip along with parents' identity proof. Source: multiple Karnataka government service guides describing the Seva Sindhu birth-certificate workflow.

## Death certificate

Death certificate issuance is one of the few Seva Sindhu services with a fully published, official fee and turnaround listed directly on the portal itself: **no additional documents required** for events already registered, an application fee of ₹5, a service charge of ₹20 when applied through a Grama One / Bangalore One (B1) / Karnataka One (K1) centre, and a stated service time of **"Immediate"** for already-registered deaths. Eligibility is open to citizens of India. Source: sevasindhu.karnataka.gov.in official Death Certificate service page. This immediate turnaround applies specifically to downloading a certificate for a death already recorded in eJanma — a death not yet registered follows a separate registration process with different (and not verified in this research) timelines.

## Income certificate

An income certificate confirming a household's annual income — required for school/college admissions, scholarships, and various subsidy schemes — can be applied for through either the **Nadakacheri** portal (also called Atalji Janasnehi Kendra, the Karnataka Revenue Department's dedicated certificate service) or through Seva Sindhu. Commonly required documents include Aadhaar card, residence proof (ration card, voter ID, passport, or driving licence), income proof (salary slip, Form 16, IT return, or a self-declaration affidavit for informal income), and a passport-size photograph. Processing time is reported consistently across independent guides as **roughly 7 to 21 working days**, depending on verification workload at the local revenue office, though some sources note certificates can occasionally be approved within 24–48 hours if all documentation is in order at first submission. **Flagged as citizen-guide-sourced, not an official portal figure** — Seva Sindhu's own published "Service Time" field for the specific Income Certificate service was not independently confirmed during this research and should be checked on the live portal before being quoted as a guarantee.

## Caste certificate

Caste certificates confirming SC/ST/OBC or other reservation-category status are available through the same Nadakacheri/Seva Sindhu channels. Seva Sindhu's own official service listing states: application fee ₹25, service charge ₹30 (₹5 if applied over-the-counter at a citizen service centre), and a published **Service Time of 21 days**. Required documents per the official listing: address proof, EPIC (voter ID), a general ID proof, an old/previous caste certificate if one exists, ration card, a relatives' certificate and genealogical tree if none of the standard documents are available, a school certificate if the applicant has studied, and a self-declaration by the applicant countersigned by the Revenue Inspector (urban). Source: sevasindhu.karnataka.gov.in official Caste Certificate service page.

## Domicile / residence certificate

Domicile (residence) certificates are also processed through Nadakacheri/Seva Sindhu, confirming an applicant's residency in Karnataka for purposes such as college admission quotas and certain government job eligibility. **UNVERIFIED — needs confirmation:** a specific official fee and service-time figure for the domicile certificate specifically (as distinct from caste and income certificates, which do have official figures above) was not independently located in this research; do not state a specific number of days or a fee to a caller without checking the live Seva Sindhu listing.

General Nadakacheri processing guidance (applicable across caste, income, domicile, and residence certificates issued from that portal) describes a typical window of **7 to 45 working days**, with faster turnaround of 24–48 hours possible when all documents are uploaded correctly at first submission and no field-level verification is required. Source: independently published Nadakacheri user guides describing the RD-number tracking and multi-level officer approval workflow (Revenue Inspector → Tahsildar/Deputy Tahsildar e-sign).

## Property tax certificate / Khata (Bengaluru-specific)

A Khata is the property ownership and tax-assessment record for a property within Bengaluru's civic limits, issued through the **e-Aasthi** portal (bbmpeaasthi.karnataka.gov.in), which continued operating under the same name and infrastructure after the BBMP-to-GBA transition. Since October 2024, e-Khata (the digital Khata) has been the standard, and since **1 July 2025 a verified e-Khata has been mandatory** for property registration under the state's Kaveri 2.0 registration system, for building-plan approvals, and effectively for home loans — a sale without one is blocked at the sub-registrar's counter. Source: multiple 2026 property-law explainer sites tracking the Kaveri 2.0 mandate, consistent with the GBA transition timeline independently confirmed for this document.

Commonly required documents for a Khata application/transfer: registered Sale Deed, Encumbrance Certificate (EC), the previous Khata (if any), the latest property tax paid receipt, Aadhaar card of the owner(s), and the BESCOM electricity account/consumer number for the property. Fees reported: a flat e-Khata **download/issuance fee of ₹125**, and a Khata **transfer (mutation) fee of 2% of the stamp duty paid at registration, subject to a minimum of ₹500**. Processing time is reported in the range of **7 to 30 working days** for a clean application with no document mismatches; one Deccan Herald report on the earlier "Licensed Private Entrepreneur" (LPE) rollout separately cites a ₹45 service fee for uploading documents plus the ₹125 issuance fee, and notes uploading typically takes about 30 minutes at an LPE-assisted centre. Source: Deccan Herald (LPE rollout coverage) and multiple independent 2026 property-services guides describing current e-Aasthi fees and timelines; cross-check the live e-Aasthi portal before quoting exact figures, since these guides explicitly note fees are "subject to revision."

The most common failure mode reported across these guides is not a missing document but a **mismatch between records** — e.g., the owner's name on the Sale Deed not matching character-for-character with Aadhaar or the existing tax record — which stalls an otherwise complete application. A voice agent collecting information for a Khata-related query should flag this as the single most useful clarifying question to ask early.
